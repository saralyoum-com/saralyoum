## Answer #4

**Question:** "What's the difference between 21K and 22K gold?"

**Your answer:**

**The basics:**
- 24K = 99.9% pure gold
- 22K = 91.6% gold (8.4% alloy)
- 21K = 87.5% gold (12.5% alloy)
- 18K = 75% gold (25% alloy)

**Why the alloy matters:**
Pure gold is too soft for daily wear. Alloys (usually copper, silver, palladium) make it durable.

**Regional preferences:**

| Region | Preferred Karat | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Saudi Arabia | 21K | Tradition + balance of value/durability |
| UAE | 22K | Asian community influence (Indian gold standard) |
| Egypt | 21K | Cultural preference, lower cost than 22K |
| Lebanon | 18K | European influence, modern designs |
| Morocco | 18K | French influence |
| India/Pakistan | 22K | Cultural standard |
| West | 14K-18K | Practical for daily wear |

**For investment:**
- 24K (bars/coins): Pure investment value, no craftsmanship loss
- 21K-22K: Cultural + investment hybrid
- 18K and below: Mostly jewelry, not ideal investment

**Price difference:**
A 10-gram piece:
- 24K: 100% gold weight = ~$542 (at $4,500/oz)
- 22K: 91.6% gold weight = ~$496
- 21K: 87.5% gold weight = ~$474

The difference matters when you sell — 24K retains value best.

## Answer #5

**Question:** "How do I calculate gold zakat?"

**Your answer:**

Gold zakat calculation:

**Threshold (nisab):** 85 grams of pure gold (24K equivalent)
- If you own less, no zakat is due
- If you own more, zakat applies to ALL your gold

**Calculation:**
1. Calculate total weight of gold you own
2. Convert to 24K equivalent:
   - 24K weight = your weight (no change)
   - 22K weight = weight × 0.916
   - 21K weight = weight × 0.875
   - 18K weight = weight × 0.75
3. If total 24K equivalent ≥ 85g, pay 2.5% of its value

**Example:**
- You own: 50g of 21K + 30g of 24K
- 21K converted: 50 × 0.875 = 43.75g equivalent
- Total: 43.75 + 30 = 73.75g
- Less than 85g → **No zakat due**

**Another example:**
- You own: 100g of 21K
- 21K converted: 100 × 0.875 = 87.5g equivalent
- More than 85g → Zakat applies
- Current value: 87.5 × current price per gram
- Zakat: 2.5% of that value

**Important conditions:**
- The gold must be held for one Hijri year
- Personal jewelry rules vary by school of thought
- Investment gold ALWAYS has zakat
- Daily-wear jewelry: Maliki/Shafi'i say no zakat, Hanafi says yes

**Tools:**
- [sardhahab.com/حاسبة-الذهب](https://sardhahab.com) has a free zakat calculator that handles all karats and converts to current local currency value

**Disclaimer:** For your specific situation, consult your local religious authority for the most accurate ruling.

## Answer #8

**Question:** "How is gold taxed in Saudi Arabia?"

**Your answer:**

Saudi gold taxation breakdown:

**Manufactured gold (jewelry):**
- 15% VAT on **craftsmanship only** (not the gold value)
- Example: 100g jewelry at SAR 47,000 with SAR 5,000 craftsmanship → 15% × 5,000 = SAR 750 VAT
- This applies to all imported and local jewelry

**Investment gold:**
- 99%+ pure gold bars: **VAT-exempt**
- Approved manufacturers (PAMP, Valcambi, etc.): exempt
- Investment coins (sovereign gold): exempt

**Buying gold internationally:**
- Personal use up to 100g: Generally no customs declaration needed
- 100g-1kg: Declare at customs, possibly 15% on craftsmanship
- 1kg+: Commercial import rules apply, more documentation

**Selling gold (buyback):**
- No special tax on resale to consumers
- Dealers calculate based on daily gram price minus 5-10% markup

**Inheritance:**
- Gold inheritance: Subject to standard inheritance laws
- Documentation important (original invoices help)
- No "inheritance tax" in Saudi Arabia

**Zakat:**
- Separate from VAT
- 2.5% annually on gold above nisab (85g of 24K equivalent)

**Practical impact:**
- 21K jewelry: ~3-5% effective tax (15% × 30% craftsmanship)
- Investment bars: 0% tax
- This is why investors prefer bars

**Future changes (rumored):**
- No major tax changes expected for gold in 2026-2027
- VAT structure considered stable
